## Applied completion rule

An alternative may satisfy the same logical section as a main route when a
specific review establishes the same connection and a comparable journey.
Physical journey credit still requires evidence of the path actually travelled;
travelling an alternative must not colour an untravelled main path as travelled.
Materially different paths do not inherit each other's completion. A separately
numbered, officially designated NCN route may be assessed in its own right.
Nearby paths do not become NCN merely because they are convenient alternatives.

No same-connection proxy is established in this review. The captured NCN228
source has two endpoints, neither close to a different officially open NCN main
route: the nearest distances in the pinned geometry are approximately 6.59 km
and 2.01 km. This is evidence against treating this captured extent as a local
two-gate bypass; it is not a claim about every possible path on the ground.

The Trust's [Sussex and Surrey map description](https://shop.walkwheelcycletrust.org.uk/products/central-sussex-and-south-surrey-cycle-map-7)
identifies National Route 228 from Faygate to Crawley. West Sussex County
Council's [LCWIP, pages 21–22](https://www.westsussex.gov.uk/media/chjdcz0u/local_cycling_and_walking_infrastructure_plan.pdf)
describes NCN228 as a less direct partial alternative to the A264 corridor and
identifies a missing link. The A264 corridor is not a parent NCN route in this
review. These sources corroborate a separately numbered NCN route, rather than
permission to invent a new route or proxy credit for a different NCN number.

## NCN626: the missing geometry is largely labelled NCN601

The refreshed official query contains the same 35 NCN601 features, 15 NCN228
features and one NCN626 diversion as the pinned snapshot. Most of the source
relation still labelled NCN626 follows the officially mapped NCN601 corridor.
The Trust's [Manchester map description](https://shop.walkwheelcycletrust.org.uk/products/merseyside-manchester-cycle-map-25)
lists National Route 601 from Oldham to Ashton-under-Lyne, while its
[Route 626 page](https://www.walkwheelcycletrust.org.uk/find-a-route-on-the-national-cycle-network/route-626/)
continues to describe two unconnected open sections under the older number.
Both map editions are May 2021; neither prose page alone establishes the
complete current identity or detailed open geometry of each source span.

NCN601 has approximately 6,316.974 m of officially open main linework in this
projection. At 10 m, about 544.317 m of the captured NCN626 source lies outside
that open-main corridor, and 141.561 m of that official linework lies outside
the source corridor. Differences persist at 25 m. The NCN601 features also
include a Temporary Closure; the approximately 81 m NCN626 feature remains a
Temporary Diversion. None of these is silently converted to open main extent.

Source way 359405104 remains tagged as construction with an old damaged-bridge
closure note. Refreshed provider linework agrees with the pinned nodes, but
that agreement is not proof that the bridge has reopened. Nor is the proposed
Park Bridge viaduct scheme proof of an available connection.

**Disposition:** keep NCN626's existing inventory and zero associations intact.
Take its source spans forward to an explicit NCN601 source-classification review.
Require a bounded, evidenced source-to-target identity decision, then reapply
current access, geometry, closure and extent checks. Do not create a blanket
626-to-601 alias, duplicate collection distance, move old completion, or invent
the missing connections described in the older Route 626 page. NCN601 was in
the missing-source-membership queue; this review supplies an identified lead,
not an approved replacement membership or a newly supported NCN601 catalogue.
